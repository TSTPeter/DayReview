The challenge

Twenty-four words. No marks, no score, nothing to get wrong. It just tells the app what to teach you.

Pencil and paper

This one is done on paper. The app reads each word out in a sentence. Write it with a pencil, then a grown-up types your answers in afterwards.

Why paper: typing makes one particular kind of spelling mistake look about six times worse than it is, and that is exactly the kind this is measuring. Handwriting gives the honest number.

    This week’s spellings

    Paste the list, or type one word per line. Numbers and punctuation are ignored.

    The words

    Test day

    Until then, about two words in three each session come from this list. After it they don’t disappear. They join the normal rotation and come back a day, then two, then four, then eight and sixteen days later. That later part is what makes them stick for the writing, rather than just for Friday.

    Grown-up view

    Practice, day by day

    Gaps are visible on purpose. This is a description for you, not a streak for her: a reset-to-zero counter is the mechanic the evidence and the ICO Children's code both warn against.

    This week’s list

    Words she has not met yet are shown plain. This is a coverage check for you, not a score for her.

    Phonological reliance

    Share of her errors that read aloud correctly. You want this falling: it means she is moving from sound to orthography. The cohort study this reproduces ran at about 84%.

    Orthographic choice

    Share of her correct spellings that needed a choice between several legal spellings of one sound. Our definition is structural, the study's was expert judgement, so do not compare this with their 14%. Watch the trend.

    Delayed accuracy

    Not session score. Accuracy 7 and 28 days after a word was first seen, which is the outcome that ladders up to a dictated test a week later.

    Transfer

    Taught words against untaught words using the same rules. A big gap means she is learning words, not rules. Expect this to lag.

    Rule strength, weakest first
    Typing fluency

    A child hunting for keys is being assessed on typing, not spelling. If this is slow, shift the balance towards paper rounds.

    Her name

    The welcome screen greets her by this name. It stays on this iPad: it is never synced, never exported, and not part of her learning record.

    Games

    Points exist only inside the games, for answers right at the first go. They open the bonus round and never reach her practice screens. The question to watch is whether the games take time from dictation practice: compare this with the calendar above.

    Which help works for her

    After a missWordsChecked a day or more later Right thenChance it is best

    Each word gets one kind of help for the whole term, picked at random, and a miss always keeps the rule. The test is her next dictation of the word a day or more later. Registered before it started, in experiments/2026-10-support-types.md: nothing changes before 6 November, whatever this table shows. With one child it can choose for her; it cannot prove anything for anyone else.

    Sound and comfort

    Comfort preferences only. There is no evidence that special fonts or coloured overlays improve reading, and this app makes no such claim.

    Saying it aloud

    When a word's help is to say it aloud, the game listens for her voice and ends the pause when it hears her. It only measures how loud the room is, on this iPad: nothing is recorded, kept, sent or turned into words. The microphone opens when the game starts, the browser shows that it is on, and it closes when she leaves the game.

    Her data

    What she types stays on this device. If sync is on, only counts and percentages leave it: never a word she wrote, never her keystrokes. The config lives only on this iPad, so no other device that opens the game syncs anything.

    Share with other parents

    A message you can send to another parent. It says what the game is and where to find it, and nothing about your child.

    Only the game’s address goes out, with no name, no results and no tracking, so nobody is told who shared it. What the game keeps is on its notice for families.
